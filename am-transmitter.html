AM Transmitter

Discrete-component AM transmitter designed and built from scratch

Overview

This project involved designing and building a functional amplitude modulation transmitter using discrete electronic components. The circuit was designed and tested in stages before being assembled onto a protoboard.

The completed transmitter successfully generates an RF carrier, modulates the carrier with an audio signal, and produces a signal that can be received using an AM radio.

Design Goals

  • Design an AM transmitter from discrete components
  • Generate a stable RF carrier
  • Introduce an audio signal through amplitude modulation
  • Amplify the RF signal
  • Construct the final circuit on a protoboard
  • Verify operation using electronic test equipment

Block Diagram

The transmitter is divided into several functional stages:

Audio Input → Modulation → RF Oscillator → RF Amplifier → Output

Schematic

RF Oscillator

The RF oscillator generates the carrier signal used for transmission. The oscillator was designed around a target carrier frequency and tested independently before being connected to the rest of the transmitter.

The frequency is primarily determined by the reactive components used in the oscillator. Component values were selected based on the desired operating frequency and then verified experimentally.

RF Amplifier

The RF amplifier increases the amplitude of the carrier signal before it is sent to the output. A buffer stage is used to reduce loading on the oscillator and help maintain stable oscillation.

AM Modulation

The audio signal is introduced into the transmitter so that the amplitude of the RF carrier varies with the audio waveform. This produces the amplitude-modulated signal that can be recovered by an AM receiver.

Construction

After testing the individual stages, the circuit was assembled onto a protoboard. Care was taken to keep the RF circuitry physically organized and to minimize unwanted coupling between different stages.

Testing and Debugging

The circuit was tested incrementally rather than assembling the entire transmitter at once. This made it possible to isolate problems within individual stages.

One of the challenges encountered during development was preventing the following amplifier stage from excessively loading the oscillator. A buffer stage was used to electrically isolate the oscillator from the rest of the transmitter.

Results

The completed transmitter successfully produced an AM signal and transmitted an audio signal that could be received using an AM radio. The final circuit was assembled entirely from discrete components on a protoboard.

What I Learned

This project gave me practical experience with RF oscillators, transistor amplifiers, impedance loading, amplitude modulation, protoboard construction, and troubleshooting analog circuits. It also demonstrated the difference between theoretical circuit behavior and the behavior of a physical RF circuit.
